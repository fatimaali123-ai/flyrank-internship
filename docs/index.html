Content Performance Prioritization Using Search and Engagement Signals

A machine-learning decision-support workflow for prioritizing content for human review.

Author: Fatima Ali

Abstract

This study asks whether historical search, traffic, engagement, content-age, and position signals can help rank content items for human review when they show signs associated with decline.

The analysis uses the pseudonymized FlyRank ML Internship content-refresh dataset and excludes identifiers and outcome-derived fields that could leak the target.

A Random Forest classifier was evaluated against a transparent baseline using a client-grouped train/test split.

On the evaluated split, the Random Forest measured Precision@20 of 1.00 and Precision@50 of 1.00, compared with 0.35 and 0.44 for the baseline.

The resulting ranked queue is intended as directional decision-support for human content review, not as an automated SEO or causal system.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content teams often have more pages to review than analysts can inspect manually. The practical decision is therefore not simply whether a page is "good" or "bad", but which pages deserve attention first.

This project evaluates whether historical search and engagement signals can support that prioritization.

The unit of analysis is an individual pseudonymized content item. The output is a ranked review priority score with reason codes and recommended human-review actions.

2. Data

The analysis uses the FlyRank ML Internship starter content-refresh dataset containing 30,000 pseudonymized content items and 44 available columns.

The target is is_declining_label, defined as 1 when trend_direction is "down" and 0 otherwise.

Identifier fields such as content_id and client_id were excluded from predictive modeling. Outcome-derived fields including trend_direction and trend_pct were also excluded to reduce leakage risk.

The final model uses 29 numeric features covering search demand, competition, content size, historical impressions and clicks, recent activity, content age, freshness, CTR, position, engagement, scrolling, and AI-traffic signals.

No client names, domains, private queries, credentials, or other client-identifying information are presented in this paper.

3. Methodology

Model

A Random Forest classifier produces a decline-risk score that is used for prioritization.

Target

The target is is_declining_label, where 1 represents content whose observed trend direction is "down".

Validation

Validation uses a client-grouped train/test split so that clients in the test set do not appear in the training set.

Baseline

The Random Forest is compared with a transparent Week-4 hand-written baseline based on impressions and CTR.

Leakage checks

Identifier fields and label-derived fields were excluded. Missing numeric values were handled using training-set medians.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • Performance describes the evaluated client-grouped test split and does not guarantee future performance.
  • The model is not causal.
  • A high model score does not prove that refreshing content will increase traffic, clicks, CTR, or rankings.
  • Performance may change when the data distribution, content mix, available fields, or business objective changes.
  • The ranked queue is a human-review prioritization tool rather than an automated publishing, deletion, SEO, or refresh system.

6. Ranked Recommendations

Priority 1 — Review high-score declining content

Items with high model scores and an observed declining trend should receive the earliest human review.

Reason code: REFRESH_DECLINING

Action: Review the content for a possible targeted refresh.

Priority 2 — Review high-score items with insufficient evidence

A high model score should not automatically trigger an edit. Reviewers should verify the underlying evidence and current content relevance.

Reason code: REVIEW_ONLY

Action: Human review before deciding on any content action.

Priority 3 — Keep content changes human-controlled

The model should not automatically publish rewrites, delete content, change URLs, modify technical SEO settings, or treat a high score as proof that a refresh will improve performance.
